Privacy Policy

Last updated 15 October 2026

Who we are

BOM collects as little as it can: no phone number, no contacts, no messages you write. The only name it stores is the display name you choose to show, if you set one. This policy covers users in the United Kingdom, Hong Kong, the United States and Taiwan.

BOM is an app for iPhone and Android for sending a friend a short, pre-written note of praise — with your name, or anonymously to friends who already use BOM. It is available in the United Kingdom, Hong Kong, the United States and Taiwan.

BOM is run by Ho Chung Au, based in the United Kingdom, who is the controller of your data under UK data protection law and the data user under Hong Kong's Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. You can contact us at bomapphelp@gmail.com.

What we collect

When you open BOM it signs you in with a random anonymous ID, not linked to your phone number, email or Apple ID unless you choose Account backup: then Apple gives BOM a random identifier for your Apple ID (no name or email), kept with your account so you can get it back on a new phone. Deleting your data removes it and ends the link with Apple. If you back up with Google instead, Google gives our sign-in service (Firebase Authentication) your Google account ID, name, email address and profile picture link; they are kept with your account only so you can get it back, BOM never shows or uses them, and deleting your data removes them. On Android, if you move to a new phone with Android's phone-to-phone transfer, BOM's data on your phone, including your sign-in, moves with it so you keep your account; it is not included in Google's cloud backups.

On our server: your anonymous ID; your display name, if you set one; for each BOM you share, which pre-written message it is, its code, when it was sent and your display name if set; for each BOM you send to a BOM friend, which message, the friend's anonymous ID and when (including the delivery time, if you schedule it) (an anonymous one carries a random tag, not your ID; the link to you is kept in a private record that only you can see in the app, and that we look at only to review a report or when the law requires it); whether each BOM was opened, thanked, skipped or reported, and when; who your BOM friends are and the BOM, QR code or invite link that linked you; your age group, 16–17 or 18 and over, as you said when you joined (your BOM friends' apps can read it, so they know what they can send you); for each “add BOM friend” QR code or invite link you make, your anonymous ID, your display name if set, and when (it works for 24 hours); and how you use the app: first and last day used, the days you used it, your phone's region setting (e.g. HK or GB), BOM's language and your time zone. If someone misuses BOM, we keep their anonymous ID in a list of banned accounts, with the reason.

To keep copies of the app that aren't genuine away from our server, Apple (App Attest) or Google (Play Integrity) confirms to it that each request comes from the real BOM app; we receive only that confirmation.

On your phone, with a backup copy on our server in a private record only you can see in the app (so they survive reinstalling BOM or a new phone): nicknames you type for recipients and BOM friends, and the senders you have blocked (their random tags, not who they are). Only on your phone: which BOMs you have opened.

You become BOM friends with someone when one of you thanks the other for a BOM they shared and leaves “Also become BOM friends” ticked, or scans the other's QR code or opens their invite link. Your BOM friends list is visible only to you.

Only on your phone, never stored or sent: what you type into “Help me pick” (Apple's on-device AI uses it to suggest a ready-made note), and the camera picture when you scan a QR code.

What we never collect

Your phone number, email address or real name (unless you type it as your display name, or back up with Google as described above). Your contacts. Any message you write: BOM has only pre-written messages; the one thing you can type is your display name, which is checked against a list of offensive words. Your location (only your phone's region setting, never GPS). Your photos or microphone (the camera is used only while you scan a friend's QR code). Advertising IDs: BOM has no ads, no third-party analytics and no cross-app tracking.

Why we use it

To deliver BOMs, show who sent a named BOM, and show you whether yours were opened or thanked (UK GDPR: contract). To stop misuse through reports, blocking, the name filter and sending limits (legitimate interests). To learn whether BOM works in each market, comparing Hong Kong and the UK using totals only, such as how many BOMs are sent and how many people come back (legitimate interests).

We never build profiles of individuals, and never sell, share or use your data for advertising or direct marketing. In Hong Kong, your data is used only for these purposes, which are directly related to why it was collected.

Where it is stored

Google Firebase (Firestore and Firebase Authentication), run by Google as our data processor, in Google's London region — for every user, so data of users in Hong Kong, the United States and Taiwan is stored outside where they live. Google may process limited technical data outside the UK under its own transfer safeguards. Every week we also save a backup copy of the server data on our own computer in the UK, kept for up to 5 weeks. We access it only from the UK, and no one else receives BOM data.

How long we keep it

We keep BOM records for 12 months from when they are created, and your BOM friends list and your usage record for 12 months from when you last used the app, then delete them within the following month. A reported BOM is kept for review for the same 12 months, even if its sender or the person who reported it deletes their data, together with the link between a reported anonymous BOM and its sender. When a sender deletes their data, the BOMs their recipients already have stay with them, anonymised, until their 12 months end. The list of banned accounts is kept while it is needed to stop misuse, and we review it at least once a year. Your private backup of nicknames and blocked senders is kept for 12 months from when you last used the app. Backup copies are replaced within 5 weeks.

Anonymity and safety

BOM is for people aged 16 and over in the United Kingdom and Hong Kong, and 18 and over in the United States and Taiwan (judged by your App Store country on iPhone, and by your SIM card's country or your region setting on Android); if we learn someone under that age is using it, we may suspend their account and ask them to delete their data. To protect 16- and 17-year-olds, BOMs that adults send them always show the sender's display name and are never about how they look.

BOMs you share yourself are not anonymous: they show your display name if set, and arrive from your own WhatsApp or messaging account. BOMs sent to a BOM friend can be anonymous: they carry no ID of yours, only a random tag, so the recipient can block you without learning who you are. We do not reveal who sent an anonymous BOM unless the law requires it.

We review reports once a week, and answer emails about abuse within 2 days. To review a report we look at the reported BOM and, for an anonymous one, who sent it; we never tell anyone who reported them, and never tell the person who reported who sent it. If someone misuses BOM, we can ban their account: they can no longer send BOMs, add BOM friends or make QR codes. Blocking is kept on your phone and in your private backup, and the sender is not told. Display names are checked against a list of offensive words in several languages; no filter is perfect, so please report any card with an offensive name.

Your rights

In the UK (UK GDPR) you can ask for a copy of your data, or to correct, delete, restrict or object to its use, and to receive it in a portable format. In Hong Kong (Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance) you can ask for a copy of your personal data (a data access request) and to correct it; we will also delete it on request. In Taiwan (Personal Data Protection Act) you can ask to see or get a copy of your data, to correct or add to it, to stop us collecting, processing or using it, and to delete it; BOM can't work without its anonymous ID. In the United States, wherever you live, you can ask to see, correct or delete your data. We don't sell or share personal information, and we don't track you across other apps or websites, so Do Not Track signals change nothing. BOM is not directed at children: no one under 16 may use it, and no one under 18 in the United States or Taiwan. Where a state's law requires app stores to check age (for example Texas), BOM uses only the age range your App Store or Google Play account shares, only to keep under-18s out.

The quickest way to delete everything is Settings → Profile → Delete my data, which removes straight away every BOM you received and every BOM you sent that nobody has received yet (except reported ones, kept for review as above), your BOM friends, your wings, your usage days, your private backup of nicknames and blocked senders, and any Apple or Google account backup. BOMs you sent that someone already has stay with them, anonymised: your anonymous ID, your display name and the tag that linked them to you are removed, so nothing on them points back to you. Without the app, see bomapp.github.io/delete.html. For other requests, email bomapphelp@gmail.com with your anonymous ID (shown in Settings → Profile). We reply within one month.

You can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office in the UK (ico.org.uk) to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data in Hong Kong (pcpd.org.hk), or to Taiwan's personal data protection authority.

Changes

If we change this policy, we will update the date above and show a notice in the app before the change applies. Questions: bomapphelp@gmail.com.
